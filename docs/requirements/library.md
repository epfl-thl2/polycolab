# Shared Library

The Library is a shared bank of statements, definitions, and questions.
Researchers discover public work and copy exact revisions into projects for further development.

## Admission and status

Any signed-in human may admit an entry.
The formulation, explicit assumptions, recorded mathematical status, and at least one supporting basis are required:

- Literature: an identified source, version, and exact locator.
- Human review: an explicit rationale attributed to the submitting human, with the time of review.
  It does not require a second reviewer.

An entry may have both bases.
Standard UI statuses are Established, Conjectured, Open, Disproved, and Definition.
Neither admission nor human review means a machine proof check.

The admitting human or an authorized moderator can revise an entry.
Revisions preserve prior statements, sources, status and review attribution.
A review of a new revision is an explicit new attestation, not inherited from an earlier one.
Agents cannot admit entries or change official Library status.
Other humans may suggest corrections.

## Discovery and development

Anonymous readers can:

- Search admitted entries by formulation, assumptions or source.
- Filter by type, status, source and status basis.
- Inspect exact revisions and history.
- Share revision links.

Library discovery includes admitted entries rather than ordinary project records.
Hidden entries remain subject to moderation when searched or opened directly.

Copying requires a signed-in human with write permission in an active destination project.
New projects default to public and may explicitly be private.
A copy is a new editable research object with its own identity and revision history.
It preserves the formulation, assumptions, explanation and an exact source revision link.
Library status, admission and verification are not inherited.
Future source updates do not change the copy, and editing the copy does not change the Library.

Retrying a completed copy must not create a duplicate.
A new copy action creates a separate object.
Creating a project with its first copy succeeds or fails together.

Existing references to exact Library revisions remain readable.
Changing a pinned reference is explicit.
New Library copy actions create independently editable objects.

Subject taxonomy and automatic literature ingestion remain future options.
